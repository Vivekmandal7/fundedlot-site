Minimum trading days in prop firms

Challenge rulesTrading glossary·Updated 24 October 2026
Definition. The minimum number of separate days with at least one trade that a prop firm requires before a phase can pass, commonly 3 to 5.

Minimum trading days are the fewest separate days on which you must trade before a prop firm phase can pass, commonly 3 to 5 per phase. Reach the profit target on day two and the phase still waits until the minimum is met.

Quick answer

A trading day usually means a day with at least one executed trade, counted in the firm's server time zone. If the minimum is 4 days, you need trades on 4 different days, whatever your profit. Some firms have no minimum at all.

What counts as a trading day

  • At least one executed position opened (sometimes opened or closed) that day.
  • Server time, not yours. Days roll over at the firm's platform midnight; late-session traders can trade "twice" in one local day or miss one.
  • Not consecutive. Days rarely have to be in a row.

Example

A $100,000 phase has an 8% target and a 4-day minimum. You make $8,200 across Monday and Tuesday. The target is met, but the phase passes only after trades on two more days. Many traders blow the account there, trading big on days three and four to "finish". The safer move is a minimum-size trade on each remaining day: it counts as a day and risks almost nothing.

FAQ

What are minimum trading days in a prop firm?

The fewest separate days on which you must place a trade before a phase can pass, commonly 3 to 5.

Does a day with one tiny trade count?

At most firms, yes: one executed trade makes it a trading day. Check whether your firm requires the trade to be open for a minimum time.

Do weekends count as trading days?

Only if you trade a market that is open and your firm counts it, such as crypto at some firms. Forex and index markets are closed at the weekend.
