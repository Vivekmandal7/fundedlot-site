FundedLot vs paper trading

Paper trading counts your reps. It never counts the cost.

Paper trading is the oldest practice tool in the book, and the book is right: reps matter. But an evaluation is not a rep count — it's a constraint system. The comparison below is really about one question: can your practice environment say no to you?

The fair reading

Where paper trading genuinely helps

Volume of practice

Hundreds of low-stakes entries build execution fluency — reading the tape, placing stops, working orders. Volume is a real teacher.

Habit formation

A daily paper session builds the routine of showing up, journalling and reviewing. Routine survives the jump to real accounts.

Strategy iteration

Testing a setup across fifty paper trades beats testing it across five real ones. Cheap iteration is the entire point of simulation.

The structural gap

Four things paper trading can't do for an evaluation

It can't end your run

Paper accounts are immortal. Blow through any loss and tomorrow looks identical. An evaluation's entire psychology comes from the opposite fact: one bad day is terminal. You can't rehearse mortality in an immortal account.

It doesn't know the rulebook

Profit targets, daily loss method, trailing floors, minimum days, consistency caps — the exam's actual grading criteria don't exist in a paper app, so you graduate fluent in the wrong test.

It reports P&L, not behaviour

Paper stats tell you that you lost. They don't detect the revenge entry, the 1.8× oversize, or the stop you quietly widened. Those patterns — not P&L — are what fail evaluations.

Its wins teach the wrong lesson

Unlimited-loss environments reward exactly the habits an evaluation punishes: averaging down, holding losers, going big to get back. Winning at paper trading can make you worse at evaluations.

Questions

Paper trading FAQ

Is FundedLot just paper trading with extra steps?

The order simulation layer is similar — virtual fills on real prices. Everything above that layer is different: an enforced rulebook, terminal breaches, behaviour detection, and a readiness estimate. The "extra steps" are the exam. Paper trading without them is practising penalty kicks with no goalkeeper.

Should I paper trade before using FundedLot?

If you've never placed a simulated order, a week of loose practice is fine — FundedLot's simulator works for that too. Switch to structured evaluations as soon as basic execution is comfortable; the earlier the rules enter your practice, the fewer bad habits you'll have to unlearn.

My paper results are great. Am I ready for a challenge?

Maybe — but paper P&L is the weakest possible evidence, because it was earned without loss limits. Run the same strategy through a rule-enforced evaluation first. If the results hold under a daily loss limit and a drawdown floor, that's real evidence. If they don't, the discovery was free.
