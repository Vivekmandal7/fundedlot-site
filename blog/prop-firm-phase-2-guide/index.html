Phase 2 of a Prop Firm Challenge: Why the Second Step Is Different

Guides 21 October 2026· 7 min read· By the FundedLot team

You passed Phase 1. The target was 8% and you made it in eleven trading days, so Phase 2 with its 5% target should be a formality. Then the second step takes four weeks and ends on a daily loss breach. This story is common enough that most firms built the second step precisely to catch it: the verification phase is not a smaller version of the first exam, it is a different one, and it is failed for different reasons.

What actually changes between the two phases

On paper, only one number moves: the profit target usually drops from around 8–10% to around 4–5%. The daily loss limit stays, the drawdown stays, and the minimum trading days usually stay. Many firms also remove the deadline in Phase 2 or make it generous. In other words, Phase 2 asks for less profit over more time with the same downside — which is a test of a completely different skill.

Phase 1 rewards the trader who can produce a burst. Phase 2 rewards the trader who can stop. The same risk per trade that reached 8% in eleven days now overshoots a 5% target in six, and the extra days you no longer need become days you fill with trades you did not plan. The industry does not publish clean numbers, but commonly cited estimates suggest a meaningful share of traders who pass Phase 1 do not complete Phase 2, and the journals we see point at the same two mistakes every time.

Mistake 1 — Keeping Phase 1 size

If 1% risk per trade got you through a 10% target, the arithmetic for a 5% target at the same size is unforgiving: a normal losing streak of four now costs most of the room you had to the target, and the daily limit has not moved. Halve the risk per trade in Phase 2 and the target is still reachable in the same number of winning trades you needed before — you simply need fewer of them in a row, and a bad day costs half as much. Sizing from the daily loss limit gives the table; in Phase 2 use the smaller end of it.

Mistake 2 — Protecting a lead with more trading

Being at 3.8% of a 5% target feels like being nearly done, and nearly-done traders press. The trade that would get you there is the one you should not take, because an evaluation is lost on the downside and never on the upside. Two rules that hold up: once past 70% of the target, cut risk per trade again; and never take a trade whose stop would bring the day inside 50% of the daily limit. The lead is yours to lose, and only a loss can lose it.

Mistake 3 — Forgetting the floor has moved (trailing drawdown)

If your firm uses a trailing drawdown, your Phase 1 profit has lifted the floor. On a $100,000 account with a 10% trailing drawdown, a Phase 1 that peaked at $109,000 may start Phase 2 with a floor at $99,000 — or the floor may reset to the new starting balance, depending on the firm. Read the rule for your account before the first trade; the trailing drawdown survival guide covers both conventions and the trades they punish.

A Phase 2 routine that fits the exam

  1. Re-plan the numbers on day one. New target in dollars, same daily limit, the floor (and whether it trails), risk per trade halved from Phase 1.
  2. Trade the same setups, fewer times. One session. Two trades a day is plenty; the no-deadline rule means a skipped day costs nothing.
  3. Bank the minimum trading days early. If the firm needs five or ten days with a trade, take them with small, planned trades rather than discovering on day 20 that you have three to go and no patience left.
  4. Cut risk twice: after two red days, and again past 70% of the target.
  5. Journal the day you did nothing. A Phase 2 journal is mostly about trades not taken. Writing down the one you skipped and why is what makes the next skip easier.

When Phase 2 keeps ending the same way

If you have failed a verification phase twice on the same kind of day — a lead given back, a daily limit hit on trade four — the information in this article is not the missing piece. You know the rule. What you need is someone to review your own trades against your own plan while the pattern is forming, not after the account is gone. The first thing a good reviewer will do is show you how many of your Phase 2 trades were in the plan. The number is usually smaller than you think, and it is the whole answer.

Rehearse the second step on its own

A simulator that enforces the rules lets you practise Phase 2 as a separate exam: same loss rules, a 5% target, no deadline, the floor where your firm puts it. Run it five times at half size before the real one.
