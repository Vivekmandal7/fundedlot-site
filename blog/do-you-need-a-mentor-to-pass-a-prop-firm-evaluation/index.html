Do You Need a Mentor to Pass a Prop Firm Evaluation?

Psychology 21 October 2026· 7 min read· By the FundedLot team

Paid trading rooms and mentorships are a large, mostly unmeasured industry, and the honest answer to the headline is: it depends on why you are failing. Some evaluation failures are information problems that a free article fixes in ten minutes. Others are pattern problems that no amount of reading touches, because the person reviewing the mistake is the person who keeps making it. This guide separates the two, and gives you a short test to apply to any room before you pay for it — including ours.

Two kinds of failure

Information failures are the ones with a rule you did not know or did not translate into dollars: the daily limit was measured on equity, the drawdown trailed, the consistency rule capped your best day. The fix is reading. Prop firm rules explained and how to pass a prop firm evaluation cover most of them, and a rule-enforced simulator makes the rest unmissable.

Pattern failures are different. You know the rule and can recite it. You still take the fourth trade on a red day, still move the stop, still size up after two losses. The journal records it; the next account repeats it. Commonly cited industry estimates put overall evaluation failure near 90%, and the traders failing for the second and third time are overwhelmingly in this group. Reading more has stopped working for them. What tends to work is a reviewer who is not them — which is what a mentor is, if the mentor is any good.

What a mentor can actually do (and what they cannot)

A good mentor can do three things a chart and a book cannot:

  • Review your trades against your plan, weekly, from your own journal — and tell you the one habit to fix next rather than ten.
  • Show the decision, not the answer. A setup written as an entry zone with an invalidation level and a target teaches you to make the decision; a price to copy teaches you nothing and, as below, can cost you the account.
  • Keep you in the routine on the days you would otherwise skip the journal or add a trade. Most of the value of a room is that someone sees what you did.

What no mentor can do is remove the risk from the trading or guarantee a pass. A room promising either is selling something other than mentorship.

The red flags that cost traders their evaluations

  1. "Copy my trades." Most prop firms forbid copy trading and what they call group trading: many accounts hitting the same price at the same firm at the same time. The firm's risk desk sees the cluster, and payouts get denied after the pass. A room that posts exact entries for members to mirror puts every member's account at risk, whatever the win rate.
  2. "Guaranteed" anything. No one can guarantee a pass on an account the firm controls.
  3. A pass rate with no denominator. "200 passes" means nothing without the number of members it came from. A room that shows you "41 passes out of 183 members" is telling you something; a room that shows a wall of certificates is telling you it has a wall.
  4. Losing calls that disappear. If outcomes are not posted for every setup, winners and losers, with a running result for the month, the track record is an advertisement.
  5. Screenshots as testimonials. Undated, uncheckable, and easy to make. A verified pass is a certificate the member uploaded, with a date, that someone checked.
  6. Onboarding by DM. Rooms that add and remove members by hand leak: people who stopped paying stay, and nobody knows who is in there. It is a small sign of a larger lack of process.

A five-question test for any room

  • Are setups written as zones with an invalidation and a target, and does the room say in writing that your entry and size are your own?
  • Is every outcome posted, including the losers, with the month's running result?
  • Does the pass count come with the member count and a start date?
  • Does the mentor review your trades, from your journal, or only talk about theirs?
  • Can you leave in one tap, and does the room then actually stop?

Five yeses is a room that is at least honest. Then the only question is whether you are the kind of failure it helps with.
